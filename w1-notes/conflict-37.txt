| `GET /api/actions?after=N&limit=M` | the ready session's [action log](#security): its requests and marks after sequence number `N` (default `0`), `{active, log, seq, more, requests, marks, dropped, refused, marksDropped}`. One sequence numbers requests and marks alike, in the order they were recorded (a request when it ended). A read gives the next of them, at most `M` together (default and most 2,000), in that order; `more` says there are others, and `seq` is where to read on from. So a reader that reads on until `more` is `false` has had everything still kept, once: `seq` is dense, so a gap in it means items were dropped. `log` names the log: a new session's starts its `seq` again, so when `log` changes, or `active` is `false`, discard what you have and read from `0`. A request is `{seq, pane, at, method, path, status, ms, bytes, aborted}`, and a mark `{seq, pane, at, type}`. **Grouping is the reader's:** a request most likely came from, of the marks no more than 300 ms after it started (`at`), its own pane's nearest within 300 ms either side, else the nearest; the earlier (then the lower `seq`) on a tie; none when no mark is that early (the landing). A mark can come after the requests it set off, so a reader groups again as marks come. Each pane keeps its last 5,000 requests, counting those `dropped`, and its last 8,000 marks; `marksDropped` counts the marks refused or no longer kept. `refused` counts what its guards refused, by reason. With no session ready, `active` is `false`, `log` is `null` and the lists are empty. Reading it is no activity |
| `GET /api/data` | the ready session's [data checks](#data-checks): `{available, active, trust, baselineAt, baselines, rebaselined, comparedAt, errors, problems, problemsCount, boot, session}`. `available` is `false` with no inspector, and `active` is `false` with no session ready. `baselines` is each pane's baseline time. `boot` and `session` are each `{complete, aligned, flags, flagsCount, base, pr, divergence}`, or `null` before a snapshot (`boot`: before the ready read). `base` and `pr` are each pane's diff, `null` for a pane with none; a span with neither has a red `unreadable` flag. `aligned` is `false` when the panes' windows differ (one's ready read failed); `complete` is `false` in either case. **The answer is bounded, at about 4 MB:** a diff lists at most 1,000 collections, with `collectionsCount`: those with a red flag first, then those with changed rows, then those with an amber flag, then any other change, so a PR's empty or count-only padding can't push a flagged one out. Each has 50 rows of each kind, 50 dropped and 50 added fields, with whole `counts`; a divergence has 50 changes a collection (`changesCount`), ranked the same way. A span has at most 500 flags, red first in the engine's order, with whole `flagsCount` (`{red, amber}`); past 20 red `unverified` flags of named collections, the rest are one flag in their place, `{collection: null, kind: 'unverified', folded: true, collections, names}`. Each pane has at most 50 `problems`, with `problemsCount`, and an error's text is cut at 500 characters. The flags, then the PR pane's diff, the divergence and the base pane's diff, each collection's own entry (its name, key and counts) included, share 2 MB a span; a collection the budget cut says `cut: true`, and so does a diff or divergence whose listing it stopped. An answer still too big to build is built without its lists: each span then has `unlisted: true`, no `base`, `pr` or `divergence`, a red `unlisted` flag and its first 20 red flags, and `flagsCount` counts the `unlisted` flag too. `errors` says, per pane, why one has no data: `boot` for the open, seeded and ready reads, each reason kept, and `session` for the latest read. Values are masked where they may be secret. The answer is built once for each state and trust, and one that fails to build even without its lists fails again at once until the state changes. `no-store`, and reading it is no activity |
